# Gradients

How descriptor gradients (`Gradient.cs`) are placed and evaluated in gradient fill layers, shape gradient fills, gradient overlays, gradient strokes and Gradient Maps. Noise gradients are in [adjustments.md](adjustments.md#noise-gradients); the effect pipeline that uses overlays and strokes is in [rendering.md](rendering.md).

`Rendering/Gradient.cs` evaluates every stop gradient (fills, overlays, strokes, Gradient Map) and `GradientFrame` resolves its placement once per region. Fitted on psd-tools files whose layers carry Photoshop's own render of the fill (`GradientTests` strips those pixels and regenerates the fill from `GdFl`).

Gradient fill geometry (`GradientSpan.CenterChord`):

- Bounds: with Align with Layer, the exact bounds of the layer's path (anchors and control points, clipped to the canvas), else the rectangle of a raster mask that hides everything outside it, else the canvas. Path coordinates are 8.24 fractions of the canvas, so an edge at 64 reads as 63.99999; the rounded-outward pixel hull (65 px) stretched the ramp by a pixel (`gradient-sizes.psd`, max 10 before). psd-tools' 32-bit `gradient-fill.psd` aligns its 90 degree ramp to the 94 rows of its mask, not the 150 of the canvas (mean 2.3 to 0.8).
- Span: the center chord through the bounds, `min(w/|cos a|, h/|sin a|)` times Scale (reference `GradientSpanBasis::CenterChord`), centered on the bounds center plus the offset.
- Snapping: Photoshop snaps the gradient line's ends to pixel centers (`floor(v) + 0.5`, with a 0.001 tolerance for the fixed-point noise). Linear runs from the snapped start of the chord to its snapped end; Radial, Reflected, Diamond and Angle run from the snapped center to the snapped end and take their direction from those two points, so the drawn angle is quantized. At a Scale other than 100%, Linear mirrors its snapped end about the snapped center. The reference keeps the exact line, which on the 4x4 colormodes files draws the 30 degree reflected fill along the wrong diagonal: Photoshop's line runs from (2.5, 2.5) to (4.5, 0.5), the 45 degree diagonal (max 115 to 2). On `gradient-styles.psd` every type at 30 degrees, at 60 degrees reversed and at -60 degrees with Scale 50% matches within the dither (max 1 to 3; Angle keeps a few seam pixels where the ramp wraps). `photoshop-shape-gradient` went from max 8, mean 1.21 to max 6, mean 0.36 against its BMP.
- Residual: the reversed 60 degree Diamond of `gradient-styles.psd` is about 5% smaller in Photoshop than the snapping predicts (max 17); no other fill shows it.

Layer-style gradients (overlays and gradient strokes, `GradientSpan.LayerProjection`) keep the reference's placement, a center snapped to `floor(c) + 0.5` and a whole-pixel half ramp `floor(span * scale / 2)` around it (calibrated on `photoshop-gradient-overlay-geometry`), with two changes that the reference's axis-aligned, five-stop calibration could not see:

- The span is the center chord, as for fills, not the corner-to-corner projection `|cos a| w + |sin a| h`. The two agree at 0 and 90 degrees; psd-tools' `layer_effects.psd` runs its 87 degree text overlay over 60 px, the layer height (region mean 7.5 to 1.0, with the easing below). The 20 degree overlay of `layer_comps.psd` (Scale 50%) improves too (region mean 5.9 to 4.6); its 12 px shapes at 135 degrees, about 20 levels off either way, do not decide it.
- Two-stop ramps ease like fills (the endpoint smoothing of `gradient_color`), color and opacity: `photoshop-overlay-zorder` went from max 13, mean 0.67 to max 1, mean 0.006 against its BMP, and the overlays of psd-tools' `effects/stroke-composite.psd` from 46% to 4% of their pixels off by more than 2 levels. Ramps with more stops already eased.

Color model (`GradientColorModel`): Photoshop mixes Classic stops in the document's own channels, then converts. CMYK documents mix ink percentages (through the document profile after the mix), grayscale and duotone documents mix gray (duotone through its inks, like its pixels), Lab documents mix L*a*b*, RGB documents with a profile that is not sRGB mix the profile's RGB, and 32-bit RGB mixes the stored linear values: the 32-bit blue-to-red quarter point is (188, 0, 130), where an sRGB mix gives (132, 0, 89). sRGB documents keep the byte mix of the reference. The model applies to every descriptor gradient (fills, overlays, strokes); the mixed ramp is sampled once into 4096 entries. On the 4x4 files the 16 and 32-bit RGB and gray fills match within 3, duotone and the newspaper-profile RGB file within 2; CMYK keeps 11 levels in its mixed midtones and Lab 10 in its stop colors, which convert the same way without a gradient.

Dither (`Dthr`) on gradient fill layers and shape gradient fills reuses the overlay helper (`DitherGradientColor`, reference `gradient_color_dithered` in `vector_raster.cpp`). Its offsets in [-1, 2] average +0.5 levels, as in the reference, and Photoshop's own noise is unknown, so against Photoshop's dithered fills a dithered render is about 0.5 levels further off on average than an undithered one.
